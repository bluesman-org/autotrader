Start here

In short: a dry run is a simulator. A real strategy sends orders to Bitvavo.

Mode What happens
DRY RUN Orders are recorded, but nothing is sent to the exchange. No real money moves. Dry runs never send email or push alerts.
REAL Orders are sent to Bitvavo as market orders. Real money moves. Fills and failures send the alerts you enabled on the settings page.

Example. A dry buy of €1,000 records a simulated buy at the candle's opening price. Your Bitvavo balance does not change. A real buy sends a market order for about €1,000; the exchange decides the fill price and charges its fee.

The main pages

  • Backtests — test rules and settings on old candles.
  • Live — create, start, stop and watch strategies.
  • History — the engine's closed round trips and the Bitvavo ledger.
  • Settings — the Bitvavo key, trading pairs, email, push devices and password.
  • API docs — the same actions through the JSON API.

Stopping is not the same as closing

Stop disconnects a strategy. It stops making new decisions. A position that is already open stays open.

Close position sells that open position. It is a separate action. An open position also stops you from archiving or deleting the strategy.

There is no automatic profit or loss stop. You decide when to stop and close. Keep an eye on the live page, or use the real-order alerts.

Reading a backtest

In short: a backtest replays old candles. The app first finds settings that work on the earlier part of the history (train), then checks those same settings on the later part it did not use (validate).

Train — first 70%
The practice exam. The app tries the parameter combinations here and ranks them.
Validate — last 30%
The real test. These candles were not used to choose the settings.

What the columns mean

The run table repeats these six columns under Train and Validate. The default split is 70/30; the form can change it.

Column Plain meaning
Trades Completed buy-and-sell round trips. More trades are more evidence, but also more fees and noise. Five to ten trades is a thin sample; fifteen or more is better.
Skipped Entry signals that could not be filled, usually because the test account did not have enough cash for the amount plus its fee. A high number means the result is less complete.
Return % Total gain or loss on the starting cash, after fees. It is not annualised.
Max DD % Max drawdown: the worst fall from a high point of the equity curve. It is the pain you would have had to sit through. Lower is better.
Win % The share of trades that made money. A low win rate can still be profitable when the winners are much bigger than the losers.
PF Profit factor: money won divided by money lost. Above 1 is profitable; infinity means there were no losing trades. A few big wins can make it look strong on a small sample.

A small worked example

One round trip. Buy €1,000 of BTC. The price rises 1%, so the gross gain is €10. The fee is 0.2% on the buy (€2) and 0.2% on the sell (about €2). The net gain is about €6.

Max drawdown. The test account reaches €10,500, then falls to €9,800. It dropped €700 from its peak, about 6.7%.

Profit factor. If the winning trades made €300 and the losing trades lost €100, the profit factor is 3.0. If both are €300, it is 1.0.

Win rate. Four winners of €80 and six losers of €40 is a 40% win rate, but the net result is still +€80. The win rate alone does not tell you whether a strategy made money.

What to look for

  • Start with validate. The app ranks the table by the train result, so scroll across to the validate side before trusting a row. A row that only looks good on train is probably overfit.
  • Check the trade count. A +20% result from three trades is luck, not evidence.
  • Check the drawdown. A strategy that made money but spent months far below its peak is harder to follow than the return suggests.
  • Read the win rate and profit factor together. Each one alone can mislead.

Example table row (made up). A strategy with 30 train trades, −1.0% train, 15 validate trades and +3.0% validate is interesting: it was slightly down in the practice period and made money on the held-out period. The real run page shows your actual numbers.

What the simulation assumes

  • It decides after a candle has closed and fills at the next candle's open.
  • It holds one position at a time. It does not short.
  • Each buy spends the fixed euro amount from the form.
  • The fee defaults to 0.20% per side, the current taker rate. Set your own rate in the form if your account tier differs.
  • The test account starts with the initial capital in the form. The returns are on that amount, not on the amount per buy.
  • The first warm-up candles on the train side are never traded; the indicators also need their own warm-up. The validate side starts with the earlier candles already behind it.
  • Any open position is closed at the last candle, so every run ends flat.
  • It does not model order-book liquidity, slippage or exchange outages.

Why the percentages can look small

The worked example uses €10,000 starting cash and buys €1,000 at a time. A +3% validation is about €300 on €10,000, not 3% on the €1,000 position. The live dry page shows profits in euros, so compare the same kind of number.

On the run page

The form chooses the market, interval, date range, amount, fees, warm-up, train share, ranking metric, indicator parameters and entry/exit rules. The run page stores the top 50 combinations, ranked on the train side. Click a row number to see that entry's individual train and validate trades. Use live opens the create form with that entry's settings filled in.

Backtest and live results can differ. See The live engine and Troubleshooting.

The live engine

In short: the engine waits for a candle to close, decides from the closed candle, and fills at the next candle's open. It holds one position at a time.

What happens on each candle

  1. The candle closes. The engine reads the market and interval from the strategy.
  2. It checks the entry rules if it is flat, or the exit rules if it has a position.
  3. If a rule fires, the fill is at the next candle's open. A dry run records that price. A real strategy sends a market order at that moment.
  4. If no rule fires, nothing happens.

Example. A 1h strategy sees an entry signal when the 14:00 candle closes. It fills at the open of the 15:00 candle. A dry run records a simulated buy; a real strategy sends the order. It does not buy at the 14:00 close.

Order statuses

Status What it means
DRY_RUN A simulated order. Nothing was sent to Bitvavo.
FILLED The exchange filled a real order, or a dry close recorded its simulated fill.
PENDING The app does not yet know the final state of a real order. It sends no new decisions for that strategy until the next start-up reconciliation settles it.
FAILED The exchange said the order will not fill, or it rejected the order. The error column says why.
REJECTED The app refused to send the order, for example because the key, balance or market minimum was not usable. No exchange order was created.
SKIPPED The engine could not read the candle for that boundary, so it did not evaluate a trade. The reason column says why.

Start, stop and restart

  • Start seeds the strategy's history, connects to Bitvavo and starts watching closed candles. It does not replay old opportunities.
  • Stop disconnects it. An open position stays open; close it separately.
  • Restart keeps the same series start (the anchor), so the indicator decisions stay consistent. Downtime is not replayed.
  • The pair and interval are fixed after the first start. Use Duplicate to try the same rules on another pair or interval.

What the live page shows

Each card shows the strategy, its position, its recent orders and whether the candle stream is connected. The page refreshes every 10 seconds. A last error means the stream or the exchange reported a problem; the strategy may still be running without receiving candles.

If a strategy is running but silent, see Troubleshooting.

Strategies

In short: a strategy is a pair, an interval, an amount and a set of MACD and Parabolic SAR rules. Dry or real is chosen when you create it and never changes.

Creating one

  • Name — a label for the live page and history.
  • Market and interval — only EUR-quoted pairs. They are fixed after the first start.
  • Amount per buy — the euro amount of each simulated or real buy, before fees.
  • Warm-up — candles the strategy never trades while the indicators settle. The default is 200, plus the indicators' own warm-up.
  • MACD — fast, slow and signal periods, default 12/26/9.
  • SAR — acceleration and maximum, default 0.02/0.2.
  • Entry and exit rules — conditions combined with ALL or ANY.

The two indicators

MACD compares two moving averages and a signal line. The histogram is the gap between the MACD line and its signal. Parabolic SAR is a line that flips above or below the price as the trend changes. They are tools for reading momentum and trend; they do not predict the future on their own.

The rule conditions

A crossover compares the current candle with the previous one. A condition with no previous candle is false.

  • MACD bullish crossover — the MACD line crosses above its signal line.
  • MACD bearish crossover — the MACD line crosses below its signal line.
  • MACD above / below signal — the lines are already on that side; no crossover needed.
  • Histogram positive / negative — the MACD line is above / below its signal line.
  • Price above / below SAR — the close is on that side of the Parabolic SAR.
  • SAR bullish / bearish crossover — the close crosses the SAR in that direction.

ALL means every condition must be true on the same closed candle. ANY means at least one must be true.

Example. Entry ALL with MACD bullish crossover and SAR bullish crossover waits until both cross on the same closed candle. Entry ANY with the same two conditions opens when either one crosses.

Dry and real copies

A dry strategy records simulated orders. A real strategy sends market orders. You cannot switch an existing strategy between the two. Create a dry copy, or use Create real copy after a dry run, and keep the same rules and sizing.

Editing, archiving and deleting

  • Only a stopped strategy can be edited. The pair and interval stay fixed after the first start; dry/real cannot change.
  • A strategy that never started and has no orders or positions can be deleted.
  • One that has run, or has any orders or positions, is archived instead. Its history stays in the History page.
  • An open position blocks both delete and archive. Close it or reconcile a manual sell first.

Closing and history

In short: stop first, then close. A manual sell made on Bitvavo can be attached to the strategy, but only a verified exchange order is accepted.

Close a position in the app

  1. Stop the strategy.
  2. Open Close position on its card.
  3. Check the current bid, the estimated sell fee and the estimated result.
  4. Confirm. A dry run records a simulated sell at the bid. A real strategy sends a market sell.

A real close is recorded as filled only when the exchange reports it filled. If it is left pending, the position stays open and the strategy stands down until the next start-up reconciliation. Do not place another close.

Example. A dry position is worth €1,012 at the current bid. The close preview shows the bid, the estimated fee and the result after both fees. Confirming records a simulated sell; your Bitvavo balance does not change.

You sold on Bitvavo yourself

Stop the strategy, open Reconcile manual close, and pick the filled sell that closed the position. The app verifies the exchange order and records it as an external trade. A sell that covers only part of the position is refused; a larger sell is prorated.

Only use this for a sell you actually made on Bitvavo's own app or website. It is not a way to create a trade.

Example. You sold 0.010 BTC on the Bitvavo app. The strategy still shows an open position of 0.010 BTC. After you pick that sell in Reconcile manual close, the app records the external sell and closes the position. It does not send a second sell.

The History page

  • Engine trades — the strategy's closed round trips: entry, exit, fees and realised profit. Archived strategies stay visible with an (archived) label.
  • Bitvavo ledger — the account's orders and fills, average-cost profit and loss, raw transactions and current prices. It is the exchange's view, not just the engine's.

The two views can differ. The engine page is the strategy's own record; the ledger page is the account's full Bitvavo history, including trades made elsewhere.

Alerts and push

In short: only real events alert you. The app can send email and a browser push. Dry runs never alert, and there is no daily heartbeat.

What sends an alert

  • A real buy or sell filled.
  • A real order failed, was rejected, or is waiting for reconciliation.
  • A strategy was held back because start-up reconciliation failed.

Email goes to the account's email address through the SMTP server on the settings page. Push goes to every browser enabled in the settings push card.

Example. A real buy fills: you get one email and one push. A dry buy fills: nothing is sent.

Test the channels

Use Send test alert for email and Send test push for push. They send one message and report a problem on the page if the channel is not configured.

Enable push on a phone

  1. Open the settings page in Chrome.
  2. Tap Enable on this device and allow notifications.
  3. Wait for 1 device subscribed. Lock the phone and use Send test push.

Android Chrome works in the browser. On an iPhone, add the site to the home screen first; iOS only delivers Web Push to an installed web app.

Chrome says “Possible spam”

That warning comes from Chrome's on-device spam filter. The app cannot switch it off. The filter is more likely to flag repeated, generic test notifications from a new site; real order alerts are specific and rare.

On the warning, tap Show notification, then Always show and Mark as safe to Chrome. Do not tap Unsubscribe. Installing the site to the home screen also gives it an app identity and helps.

Example. The warning shows “Possible spam” with two buttons. Tap Show notification, then Always show → Mark as safe to Chrome. The next notification should show normally.

If a warning still appears on real order alerts after that, check the site permission under Chrome → Page info → Permissions → Notifications.

If the VAPID key pair is rotated

Rotating the key pair makes the old push subscriptions unusable. On each device, open the settings page, disable notifications, then enable them again. The key pair is generated once and kept encrypted in the database.

Settings and security

In short: one account, one system-wide Bitvavo key, and a key that can trade but never needs withdrawal permission.

The Bitvavo key

  • The live engine and the ledger use the same system-wide key from the settings page.
  • It is stored encrypted and shown only as a hint. Replacing it overwrites the stored key.
  • It needs read permission and spot trading permission. It should not have withdrawal permission.
  • If Bitvavo gives you an operator id, store it here so orders carry it.

Example. A key with read-only permission can load balances and history, but a real order will fail. A key with trade permission can place orders. Withdrawal permission is not needed by anything in this app.

Trading pairs

The forms and the ledger offer the pairs in the catalogue. Refresh from Bitvavo reads the account's trade history and adds or updates the pairs it finds. You can add a pair by hand; disabling one hides it but keeps its history.

Email

Recovery mail and live alerts use the SMTP server on this page. Leave the password blank when editing to keep the stored one. Use Send test alert to prove it.

Push devices

The push card lists how many devices are subscribed. Enable each device separately, and use Send test push to prove it. A browser that is gone is removed automatically; other failures keep the subscription and are logged.

Account and API

  • There is one account. The pages use a signed-in session; the JSON API uses HTTP Basic with the same account.
  • Change the password here; other sessions keep running until they are used again.
  • In production, the app is served over HTTPS. The TradingView webhook stays open because it authenticates itself.
  • The API documentation is at API docs.

Troubleshooting

In short: check the live page first for the strategy's state and last error.

My strategy is running but nothing happens.

The engine only acts when a candle closes. A 1h strategy can be quiet for an hour. Check that the card says it is connected, and read the last error. If the stream is disconnected, wait for the reconnect; if there is an error, the reason is on the card. The seed and downtime between runs are never replayed. A candle that closes during a brief stream outage is evaluated when the stream reconnects; only a boundary whose candle cannot be resolved becomes a SKIPPED row.

I get no email or push.

For email: check the account email and the SMTP settings, then use Send test alert. For push: check that the device is subscribed, that Chrome has notification permission, and that the test push works. If Chrome shows “Possible spam”, mark the site safe. See Alerts and push.

An order is PENDING. What do I do?

Do not place another close or a second order. The app reconciles pending real orders at the next start-up. Restart the application and let it settle. The strategy stands down until the order is reconciled.

Why does the live result differ from the backtest?

The live strategy has its own start point (the anchor) and never replays the seed or downtime. A candle that closes during a brief stream outage is evaluated when the stream reconnects; only a boundary whose candle cannot be resolved becomes a SKIPPED row. The backtest may have started from a different point, with different warm-up or a different position at the start. Fees and fills also differ slightly because live fills use the exchange's report.

Why can't I archive or delete a strategy?

It is running, or it has an open position. Stop it first. If the position is open, close it or reconcile the manual sell that closed it. An open position blocks both actions.

Why is a backtest row last, or why does it have no trades?

A combination with no train trades is ranked last. Check the date range, the warm-up, the amount versus the starting cash, and the rules. A row with few trades may simply not have enough opportunities in that period.

The API answers 401.

The API uses HTTP Basic with the account credentials. The pages use a session. Check the credentials, and see the API docs for the endpoint. CSRF is off for `/api/**`; that is expected.

The close page says the current price is unavailable.

The app will not guess a price. Wait a moment and try again. If the exchange cannot be reached, the close cannot run.

The engine history and Bitvavo ledger do not match.

The engine page shows the strategy's closed round trips. The ledger page shows the whole Bitvavo account, including trades made in the app, by the legacy webhook, or by hand. Average-cost profit and the engine's round-trip profit are different calculations.

Chrome says a notification may be spam.

Tap Show notification, then Always show → Mark as safe to Chrome. Do not tap Unsubscribe. See Alerts and push.

Glossary

Candle
One time period of price data: open, high, low and close.
Closed candle
A candle whose period has ended. The engine never decides on a candle that is still forming.
Warm-up
The first candles a strategy does not trade while the indicators settle.
Anchor
The fixed start of the strategy's candle series. A restart keeps the same anchor.
Round trip
One buy and the matching sell. The backtest and history count these as trades.
Dry run
A simulated order. Nothing is sent to the exchange.
Real
A strategy that sends market orders with real money.
Train / validate
The earlier part of the history used to choose settings, and the later part used to test them without changing them.
Max drawdown
The worst fall from a high point of the account value, in percent.
Profit factor
Money won divided by money lost. Above 1 is profitable.
Win rate
The share of trades that made money. It says nothing about their size.
Reconciliation
Checking a real order against the exchange at start-up, so an unknown order is settled rather than guessed.
Taker fee
The fee for an order that takes liquidity immediately. Market orders pay it.
Operator id
The numeric id Bitvavo gives an API key or account for order attribution.
VAPID
The key pair the app uses to sign Web Push messages. Rotating it invalidates existing browser subscriptions.
PWA
A website installed to the home screen as an app. On iOS, Web Push needs this.
